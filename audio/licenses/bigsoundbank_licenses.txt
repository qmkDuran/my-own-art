Content ID claim?
Sounds marked “Free and royalty-free” are not registered in Content ID or in any other recognition system. I claim nothing on the videos or tracks that use them.
What is Content ID?
Content ID is YouTube's automatic recognition system. Rights holders (labels, music distributors, artists) upload their works to it, and YouTube compares every new video against this database. When it recognizes a passage, it issues a claim on behalf of whoever uploaded the work: that party claims ownership of the passage. They can then collect the video's ad revenue, or have it blocked.
Other platforms use similar systems, as do some music distributors (TuneCore, for example), which scan a track before releasing it on streaming services.
Who is claiming, and why?
These systems do not check who actually made a sound: they trust whoever uploaded the work. The most common case: a musician or video creator uses one of my sounds (rain, thunder, ambience...) in their work, then has it registered in Content ID. My sound then becomes part of their fingerprint. Any other video or track using the same sound is then recognized as a copy of their work, and the claim is made in their name.
Such a claim is unfounded. This third party holds rights to their own work, not to my recording. These sounds are released under CC0, and I have never licensed them exclusively to anyone.
